= Tutorial 8: A waffle dome
:page-id: tutorial-waffle
include::_attributes.adoc[]

[.lead]
A waffle structure, as used for pavilions and plywood furniture: a smooth form sliced by two families of parallel planes into flat ribs that interlock. You will loft a dome, close it into a solid, cut it with a list of planes, and thicken the cuts into ribs.

image::waffle-render.png[A plywood dome made of two families of interlocking ribs]

Reference: choose menu:Examples[Waffle dome] on the canvas toolbar to load the finished definition.

== Build it

=== The dome

. Place three sliders: _Rings: 10 (4 – 16)_ with Decimals `0`, _Ring spacing: 3.2 (1 – 8)_ and _Radius: 34 (10 – 50)_.
. Place two *Series* (Sets), _Ring no._ and _Heights_. Wire *Rings → C* on both and *Ring spacing → N* on Heights. Ring no. counts 0 … 9; Heights runs 0, 3.2 … 28.8.
. Place an *Expression* (Maths), _Profile_, and set value its *F* to `z*sqrt(1 - (x/(y - 0.6))**2)`. Wire *Ring no. → x*, *Rings → y* and *Radius → z*. This is a quarter ellipse: the full radius at the ground, shrinking faster and faster towards the top. The `0.6` keeps the top ring small but open, so the loft always has a real circle to end on.
. Place a *Circle* (Curve). Wire *Profile R → R* and *Heights → Z*: ten stacked rings.
. Place a *Loft* (Surface), wire *Circle C → C* and tick menu:Flatten[] on its *C* grip.
. Place a *Cap Holes* (Surface) and wire *Loft L → B*. It closes the bottom and top with flat faces, turning the open loft into a solid that can be cut.

=== The cutting planes

[start=7]
. Place two sliders, _Ribs: 9 (3 – 21)_ with Decimals `0` and _Rib pitch: 7 (3 – 15)_.
. Place an *Expression*, _First rib_, with *F* set to `-(x - 1)*y/2`. Wire *Ribs → x* and *Rib pitch → y*. For 9 ribs 7 mm apart the first sits at -28 mm, so the ribs are centred on the dome.
. Place a *Series*, _Rib offsets_. Wire *First rib R → S*, *Rib pitch → N* and *Ribs → C*: -28, -21 … 28.

=== Ribs

[start=10]
. Place two *Section* (Surface) components. Wire *Cap Holes B → S* and *Rib offsets → D* on both. Set value *N* to `1, 0, 0` on the first and `0, 1, 0` on the second. Each Section now makes nine flat regions: the dome cut by nine parallel planes.
. Place a slider _Thickness: 1.4 (0.4 – 3)_ with Decimals `2`, and a *Multiplication* (Maths) with *B* set to `-0.5`. Wire *Thickness → A*.
. Place two *Move* (Transform) components. Wire the first Section's *R → G* and *Multiplication R → X* on one, and the second Section's *R → G* and *Multiplication R → Y* on the other. Each region steps back half a thickness, so the rib will be centred on its plane.
. Place two *Vector XYZ* (Vector). Wire *Thickness → X* on the first and *Thickness → Y* on the second.
. Place two *Extrude* (Surface) components: the first Move and the first vector into one, the second Move and the second vector into the other. These are the ribs.
. Turn menu:Preview[] off on Circle, Loft, Cap Holes, both Sections and both Moves.

== What is happening

*Section* takes one solid but a list of offsets, so longest-list matching repeats the dome for every offset and returns one region per plane. Two Sections with perpendicular normals give the two rib families.

A region is flat, with no thickness. Moving it back by half the thickness and extruding it by the full thickness along the plane's normal turns it into a rib whose middle lies exactly on the cutting plane, so the two families stay symmetric.

The ribs overlap where they cross. To make a kit you can assemble, you would cut a half-depth slot into each rib at every crossing; this example leaves that out. Cutting a solid nine times in each direction is the slow part: expect each slider step to take about two seconds.

== Things to try

* Set *Ribs* to 15 and *Rib pitch* to 4 for a dense, fine waffle (twice as many cuts, so about twice as slow).
* Raise *Ring spacing* to 6 for a tall egg instead of a dome.
* Change the profile to `z*(1 - 0.7*x/y)` for a cone, or to `z*(1 + 0.25*sin(2*pi*x/y))` for a wavy vase.
* Bake the two Extrudes and give them a plywood colour; then use FreeCAD's TechDraw or a DXF export to lay the ribs out for a laser cutter.
