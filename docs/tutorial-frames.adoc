= Tutorial 17: A frame tunnel
:page-id: tutorial-frames
include::_attributes.adoc[]

[.lead]
A row of identical rectangular frames, each turned a few degrees more than the last and swelling towards the middle, makes a twisting tunnel, like the timber "frame in the frame" pavilions built by architecture schools. One Boolean makes one frame; three transforms copy it forty times.

image::frames-render.png[A twisting tunnel of timber frames]

Reference: choose menu:Examples[Frame tunnel] on the canvas toolbar to load the finished definition.

== Build it

=== One frame

. Place seven sliders: _Frames: 40 (5 – 80)_ with Decimals `0`, _Spacing: 5 (2 – 12)_, _Width: 40 (10 – 80)_, _Height: 30 (10 – 60)_, _Frame bar: 2.5 (0.5 – 6)_ and _Swell: 0.3 (0 – 0.8)_ with Decimals `2`, and _Turn per frame: 4.5 (0 – 15)_.
. Place a *Center Box* (Surface), _Outer_, and wire *Width → X*, *Frame bar → Y* and *Height → Z*. The box is centred in X and Y and stands on Z = 0, so it is a thin upright slab.
. Place two *Expression* (Maths) components with *F* set to `x - 2*y`. Wire *Width → x* on one and *Height → x* on the other, and *Frame bar → y* on both: the opening's width and height.
. Place a *Multiplication* (Maths) with *B* set to `3` and wire *Frame bar → A*: the opening is thicker than the slab so it cuts cleanly.
. Place a second *Center Box*, _Opening_, with the two Expressions into *X* and *Z* and the Multiplication into *Y*. Place a *Move* (Transform) and wire *Opening → G* and *Frame bar → Z*, lifting it into the slab.
. Place a *Solid Difference* (Intersect). Wire *Outer → A* and *Move G → B*: one frame.

=== Forty frames

[start=7]
. Place a *Multiplication* with *B* set to `0.5` and wire *Height → A*. Place a *Construct Point* (Vector), _Centre_, and wire the Multiplication into *Z*: the frame's centre.
. Place a *Series* (Sets), _Frame no._, and wire *Frames → C*.
. Place a *Multiplication* and wire *Frame no. → A* and *Turn per frame → B*: an angle per frame.
. Place an *Expression*, _Size_, with *F* set to `1 + z*sin(pi*x/y)`. Wire *Frame no. → x*, *Frames → y* and *Swell → z*.
. Place a *Scale* (Transform). Wire *Difference R → G*, *Size → F* and *Centre → C*: forty frames, growing towards the middle.
. Place a *Rotate* (Transform). Wire *Scale G → G*, the angles *→ A* and *Centre → C*, and set value its axis *X* to `0, 1, 0`: each frame turns in its own plane.
. Place a *Multiplication* and wire *Frame no. → A* and *Spacing → B*. Place a *Move* and wire *Rotate G → G* and the Multiplication *→ Y*: the frames step along the tunnel.
. Turn menu:Preview[] off on everything except the last Move.

== What is happening

The Boolean runs once, on one frame. Scale, Rotate and Move then receive one frame and lists of 40 sizes, angles and distances, and longest-list matching repeats the frame for every one of them. That is why the tunnel solves in a few milliseconds; drawing the 40 frames takes about a third of a second. Copying and moving a solid is cheap, cutting one is not.

Scaling and rotating about the frame's own centre, rather than the origin, keeps each frame on the tunnel's axis. Scale is uniform, so a frame scaled by 1.3 has 1.3³ times the volume; the geometry tests check exactly that.

== Things to try

* Set *Turn per frame* to 0 for a plain tunnel, or to 12 for a tight corkscrew.
* Set *Swell* to 0.8 for a pronounced barrel.
* Raise *Frames* to 80 and lower *Spacing* to 2.5 for a dense, almost solid tube.
* Make *Width* and *Height* equal for a square tunnel: the twist then reads as a smooth spiral.
