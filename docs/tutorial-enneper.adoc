= Tutorial 29: Enneper's minimal surface
:page-id: tutorial-enneper
include::_attributes.adoc[]

[.lead]
A minimal surface is what a soap film makes: at every point it curves as much one way as the other. Alfred Enneper found this one in 1864, and it has a simple formula in two parameters _u_ and _v_. You will sample it on a grafted grid, fit a smooth surface through the samples and slice it into contour lines.

image::enneper-render.png[A teal saddle-shaped minimal surface with gold contour lines]

Reference: choose menu:Examples[Enneper surface] on the canvas toolbar to load the finished definition.

== Build it

=== The parameter grid

. Place four sliders: _Parameter range: 1.2 (0.5 – 2)_ with Decimals `2`, _Grid: 30 (10 – 50)_ and _Contours: 12 (2 – 40)_ with Decimals `0`, and _Size: 20 (5 – 40)_.
. Place a *Multiplication* (Maths) with *B* `-1` after *Parameter range*, and two *Range* (Maths) components, _u_ and _v_, from −range to +range with *Grid → N*.

=== The formula

[start=3]
. Place three *Expression* (Maths) components:
** _X_: `z*(x - x**3/3 + x*y**2)`;
** _Y_: `z*(y - y**3/3 + y*x**2)`;
** _Z_: `z*(x**2 - y**2)`.
** On all three wire *u → x*, *v → y* with menu:Graft[] on *y*, and *Size → z*.
. Place a *Construct Point* (Vector) with *X*, *Y* and *Z*: 31 rows of 31 points.
. Place an *Addition* (Maths), *Grid* + `1`, and a *Surface From Points* (Surface): the points *→ P* with menu:Flatten[], the Addition *→ U*.

=== Contours

[start=6]
. Place an *Expression*, `0.95*z*x**2`, with *Parameter range → x* and *Size → z*: just inside the highest point, so the top level is not a single point. Place a *Multiplication* with *B* `-1` after it.
. Place a *Range*, _Levels_, from the negative to the positive value with *Contours → N*, and a *Contour* (Curve) with *Surface → S* and *Levels → D*.
. Turn menu:Preview[] off on Construct Point.

== What is happening

The formula comes from the Weierstrass–Enneper representation, which turns simple complex functions into minimal surfaces. The surface is a saddle whose edges curl over as _u_ and _v_ grow. It passes through itself once a point lies more than √3 from the origin in (_u_, _v_); the corners of the square grid lie √2 × the range out, so the surface stays clean up to a range of about 1.22. The geometry tests check that the fitted surface passes through points computed straight from the formula.

Each update takes about a second, mostly the contours.

== Things to try

* Raise *Parameter range* to 1.8 and watch the flaps cross; lower it to 0.6 for a gentle saddle.
* Raise *Contours* to 30 for a dense topographic look.
