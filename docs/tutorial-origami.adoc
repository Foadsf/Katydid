= Tutorial 24: A Miura-ori fold
:page-id: tutorial-origami
include::_attributes.adoc[]

[.lead]
Miura-ori is the fold used for solar panels on satellites: a sheet of identical parallelograms that collapses in one motion. Geometrically it is rows of zigzags, each shifted alternately left and right. A *Loft* with *Straight* faces between the rows makes every facet a flat parallelogram, so the creases stay crisp and catch the light.

image::origami-render.png[A white Miura-ori folded sheet with alternating light and shaded facets]

Reference: choose menu:Examples[Miura-ori fold] on the canvas toolbar to load the finished definition.

== Build it

=== Points of every row

. Place six sliders: _Pleats: 16 (4 – 40)_ and _Rows: 12 (2 – 30)_ with Decimals `0`, _Pleat width: 6 (2 – 12)_, _Pleat depth: 6 (0 – 10)_, _Row height: 6 (2 – 12)_ and _Shear: 2.5 (0 – 6)_.
. Place two *Addition* (Maths) components with *B* `1` after *Pleats* and *Rows*, and two *Series* (Sets) after them: _Point no._ (0 … 16) and _Row no._ (0 … 12).
. Place a *Multiplication* (Maths): *Point no. → A*, *Pleat width → B*.
. Place an *Expression* (Maths), _Row shift_, `y*(x % 2)`, with *Row no. → x* (menu:Graft[] on *x*) and *Shear → y*: odd rows shift right by Shear. One branch per row.
. Place an *Addition*, _X_, and wire the Multiplication *→ A* and *Row shift → B*.
. Place an *Expression*, _Zigzag_, `y*(x % 2)`, with *Point no. → x* and *Pleat depth → y*: odd points step back by the depth.
. Place a *Multiplication*, _Z_: *Row no. → A* with menu:Graft[], *Row height → B*.
. Place a *Construct Point* (Vector) with *X*, *Zigzag → Y* and *Z*: 13 branches of 17 points.

=== The sheet

[start=9]
. Place a *Polyline* (Curve) and wire *Construct Point → V*: one zigzag per row.
. Place a *Loft* (Surface). Wire the polylines *→ C*, tick menu:Flatten[] on *C*, and set value *St* (Straight) to `True`.
. Turn menu:Preview[] off on Construct Point and Polyline.

== What is happening

Each row is the one below translated by (±Shear, 0, Row height). A face between two rows joins a segment of one zigzag to the matching, translated segment of the next, and four points made of a segment and its translate always lie in one plane: a parallelogram. The alternating sign of the shift makes the second set of creases. The geometry tests check that every facet is flat to a millionth of a millimetre.

A normal loft would smooth through the rows and round the creases off; *Straight* joins each section to the next with ruled faces and keeps them sharp. The sheet updates in about a sixth of a second.

== Things to try

* Set *Shear* to 0: the sheet becomes a simple pleated fan with creases in one direction only.
* Set *Pleat depth* to 0: the sheet goes flat (with the shear still visible as a skew).
* Raise *Shear* to 6 for steep, deep folds.
* Untick *St* on the Loft and watch the creases melt into waves.
