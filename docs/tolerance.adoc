= Tolerance analysis
:page-id: tolerance

[.lead]
The *Tolerance* tab answers two everyday precision-engineering questions: given a chain of toleranced dimensions, how much can the result vary? And given a feature of size toleranced at MMC, how much position tolerance does it really get, and what are its worst-case boundaries?

== Linear stack-ups

A *stack-up* follows a chain of dimensions from one side of a gap (A) to the other (B). Katydid follows the method of B. R. Fischer, _Mechanical Tolerance Stackup and Analysis_ (2nd ed., CRC Press, 2011), chapters 4, 7 and 8.

=== Dimensions

Each *Dimension* component holds one link of the chain:

[cols="1,4"]
|===
|*N* Nominal |The drawing value.
|*U* Upper, *L* Lower |The deviations, with their signs: `10 +0.2/−0.1` is U = `0.2`, L = `-0.1`.
|*D* Direction |`1` if the dimension runs from A towards B, `-1` if it runs back.
|*S* Distribution |`normal`, `uniform` or `triangular`; used only by Monte Carlo.
|*Nm* Name |The label in reports.
|===

Every dimension is first converted to *equal-bilateral* form, a mean value with a symmetric tolerance (Fischer, ch. 4):

[stem]
++++
\text{mean} = N + \frac{U + L}{2}, \qquad t = \frac{U - L}{2}
++++

So `8.50 +.25/−.10` becomes `8.575 ± .175`, and a limit dimension `9.55 – 10.00` becomes `9.775 ± .225`. The *Dimension* outputs *M* and *T* show these two numbers.

=== Worst case, RSS and adjusted RSS

The *Stack-up* component takes the whole chain (list access, so wire every Dimension into its *D*, adding wires with kbd:[Shift]) and computes, with *d* = ±1 the direction of each dimension:

[stem]
++++
\text{nominal} = \sum d_i\,\text{mean}_i \qquad
T_{\text{worst}} = \sum t_i \qquad
T_{\text{RSS}} = \sqrt{\sum t_i^2} \qquad
T_{\text{adj}} = F \cdot T_{\text{RSS}}
++++

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
|Result |How to read it
|Worst case |Every dimension at its limit in the unluckiest direction at once. The gap can never fall outside these limits.
|RSS |Root-sum-square: the probable range when dimensions vary independently and their processes are centred, each tolerance being the same number of standard deviations.
|Adjusted RSS |RSS multiplied by a factor *F* (1.5 by default, as in Fischer's examples) to allow for processes that are not perfectly centred or independent.
|===

The *Report* output lists every dimension with its *share* of the worst-case total, which shows at a glance which tolerance to tighten first, followed by the three results with their minimum and maximum.

=== Monte Carlo

*Monte Carlo Stack-up* builds the assembly many times with random dimensions (10,000 by default) and reports the mean, the standard deviation, the percentage of builds outside the optional spec limits *LSL* and *USL*, and a text histogram.

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
|Distribution |How a dimension with tolerance ±t is drawn
|normal |Mean ± t is *k* standard deviations (*k* = 3 by default, input *k*)
|uniform |Equally likely anywhere in mean ± t
|triangular |Most likely at the mean, falling linearly to mean ± t
|===

The same *Seed* gives the same builds every time, so results are reproducible; change it to see how much the reject rate varies between samples. When numpy is available (FreeCAD bundles it) the simulation is about 50 times faster than the pure-Python fallback; the two use different random streams, so a seed reproduces results only on the same one.

TIP: With every tolerance normal and *k* = 3, the Monte Carlo standard deviation comes out close to RSS / 3, and about 0.27 % of builds fall outside nominal ± RSS. That is a good sanity check of a model.

== GD&T calculators

The three GD&T components follow G. R. Cogorno, _Geometric Dimensioning and Tolerancing for Mechanical Design_ (3rd ed., McGraw-Hill, 2020), chapter 3, for features of size.

=== Bonus Tolerance

With an MMC modifier (Ⓜ) in the feature control frame, a feature gains *bonus tolerance* as its actual size departs from MMC towards LMC:

[stem]
++++
\text{bonus} = |\,\text{actual} - \text{MMC}\,| \qquad \text{total} = \text{geometric tolerance} + \text{bonus}
++++

With an LMC modifier the bonus is measured from LMC instead; with RFS (no modifier) there is no bonus. An actual size outside the MMC–LMC range is an error: that part is rejected, not given a bonus. The component's results match Cogorno's Table 3-2 row by row (see the xref:tutorial-gdt.adoc[GD&T tutorial]). To see the bonus in 3D, load the xref:tutorial-flange.adoc[flange with tolerance zones]: each bolt hole's zone grows as you drag its actual size.

=== Virtual & Resultant Condition

For a feature toleranced at MMC, with size tolerance = |LMC − MMC|:

[cols="1,2,2",options="header"]
|===
| |Internal feature (hole) |External feature (pin)
|Virtual condition |MMC − tolerance |MMC + tolerance
|Resultant condition |LMC + tolerance + size tolerance |LMC − tolerance − size tolerance
|Inner boundary |virtual condition |resultant condition
|Outer boundary |resultant condition |virtual condition
|===

The virtual condition is the constant boundary a mating part must clear (the size of a functional gauge pin or hole). The resultant condition is the opposite worst case, used for minimum wall thickness.

=== Position to ±

A stack-up needs ± values, while a drawing gives a diametral position tolerance. *Position to ±* converts them as Fischer does: ± position = position tolerance / 2, and the bonus available from a size tolerance becomes ± size tolerance / 2.

== Limits

Stacks are one-dimensional: every dimension acts along the same direction. Two-dimensional and angular stacks, datum feature shift and datum reference frames are on the roadmap. The Monte Carlo model assumes independent dimensions.
