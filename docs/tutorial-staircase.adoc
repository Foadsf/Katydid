= Tutorial 12: A spiral staircase
:page-id: tutorial-staircase
include::_attributes.adoc[]

[.lead]
A spiral staircase is one tread, repeated: each copy a little higher and turned a little further round a column. Add balusters at the outer ends and a handrail through their tops and you have a complete model in twenty components.

image::staircase-render.png[An oak spiral staircase round a white column, with a black handrail and balusters]

Reference: choose menu:Examples[Spiral staircase] on the canvas toolbar to load the finished definition.

== Build it

=== Step numbers, heights and angles

. Place six sliders: _Steps: 22 (6 – 40)_ with Decimals `0`, _Rise: 3.2 (2 – 5)_, _Turn per step: 16 (5 – 30)_, _Column radius: 3 (1 – 8)_, _Step width: 18 (8 – 30)_ and _Rail height: 15 (8 – 25)_.
. Place a *Series* (Sets), _Step no._, and wire *Steps → C*: 0 … 21.
. Place two *Multiplication* (Maths) components, _Heights_ and _Angles_. Wire *Step no. → A* on both, *Rise → B* on Heights and *Turn per step → B* on Angles.

=== Treads

[start=4]
. Place a *Center Box* (Surface). Wire *Step width → X* and set value *Y* to `8` and *Z* to `1.2`. This is one tread, centred on the origin in plan.
. Place an *Expression* (Maths) with *F* set to `x + y/2`. Wire *Column radius → x* and *Step width → y*: how far out the tread's centre sits.
. Place a *Move* (Transform). Wire *Box B → G*, *Expression R → X* and *Heights → Z*: 22 treads stacked beside the column.
. Place a *Rotate* (Transform). Wire *Move G → G* and *Angles → A*. The axis defaults to Z through the origin, so each tread swings round the column.

=== Column

[start=8]
. Place an *Expression* with *F* set to `(x - 1)*y + z` and wire *Steps → x*, *Rise → y* and *Rail height → z*: the height of the top of the handrail.
. Place a *Cylinder* (Surface). Wire *Column radius → R* and the Expression's *R → L*.

=== Balusters and handrail

[start=10]
. Place an *Expression* with *F* set to `x + y - 1.2` and wire *Column radius → x* and *Step width → y*: the radius of the rail, just inside the tread ends.
. Place two *Expression* components with *F* set to `z*cos(radians(x*y))` and `z*sin(radians(x*y))`. Wire *Step no. → x*, *Turn per step → y* and the rail radius *R → z* on both.
. Place an *Addition* (Maths) and wire *Heights → A* and *Rail height → B*.
. Place two *Construct Point* (Vector) components, _Foot_ and _Head_. Wire the cos and sin Expressions into *X* and *Y* of both, *Heights → Z* on Foot and *Addition R → Z* on Head.
. Place a *Line* (Curve) and wire *Foot → A* and *Head → B*: one baluster per step. Place a *Pipe* (Surface) with *R* set to `0.3` and wire *Line L → C*.
. Place an *Interpolate* (Curve) and wire *Head Pt → V*: a smooth curve through the top of every baluster. Place a *Pipe* with *R* set to `0.6` and wire *Interpolate C → C*.
. Turn menu:Preview[] off on Box, Move, Foot, Head, Line and Interpolate.

== What is happening

Three lists of 22, step numbers, heights and angles, drive everything. Move and Rotate pair one tread with each height and each angle, and the cos/sin Expressions turn the same step numbers into baluster positions, so a baluster always stands on its own tread. Because the handrail is interpolated through the baluster heads, it follows any change to the stair automatically.

Note that *Center Box* is centred in X and Y but stands on Z = 0, so each tread's top is 1.2 above its step height. Each update takes about a third of a second, nearly all of it drawing the preview.

== Things to try

* Drag *Turn per step*: at 30° the stair turns a full circle in 12 steps.
* Set *Steps* to 40 for a tall tower stair; the column and handrail follow.
* Raise *Column radius* to 8 for an open well staircase.
* Change the handrail's Pipe radius, or set the baluster Pipe radius to 0.15 for a lighter look.
* Bake the Rotate, Cylinder and both Pipes, colour them oak, white and black, as in the picture above.
