= Tutorial 22: A network-arch bridge
:page-id: tutorial-bridge
include::_attributes.adoc[]

[.lead]
Twin arches lean towards each other over a cambered deck, each tied to it by two families of crossing hangers: a _network arch_, the form engineers use for light, stiff spans. Three-point arcs give the arch and the deck, *Evaluate Curve* places the hangers, and *Mirror* makes the twin.

image::bridge-render.png[A white network-arch bridge with two leaning arches and a web of crossing hangers]

Reference: choose menu:Examples[Network-arch bridge] on the canvas toolbar to load the finished definition.

== Build it

=== The arch

. Place eight sliders: _Span: 120 (60 – 200)_, _Rise: 40 (15 – 70)_, _Lean: 6 (0 – 15)_, _Deck width: 16 (8 – 30)_, _Camber: 3 (0.5 – 10)_, _Hangers: 24 (6 – 48)_ with Decimals `0`, _Hanger slope: 0.08 (0 – 0.12)_ with Decimals `3`, and _Arch radius: 1.4 (0.4 – 3)_ with Decimals `2`.
. Place three *Expression* (Maths) components:
** _Spring X_, `x/2 + 6`, with *Span → x*: the arch springs 6 m beyond each end of the deck.
** _Edge Y_, `-x/2`, with *Deck width → x*: the deck's edge.
** _Apex Y_, `z - x/2`, with *Deck width → x* and *Lean → z*: the crown leans in by Lean.
. Place a *Multiplication* (Maths) with *B* `-1` after Spring X. Place three *Construct Point* (Vector) components with their *Z* set as given:
** _Spring 1_ (−Spring X, Edge Y, *Z* `-5`).
** _Apex_ (0, Apex Y, *Rise → Z*).
** _Spring 2_ (Spring X, Edge Y, *Z* `-5`).
. Place an *Arc 3Pt* (Curve) and wire *Spring 1 → A*, *Apex → B*, *Spring 2 → C*.

=== The deck

[start=5]
. Place an *Expression*, _Half span_, `x/2` with *Span → x*, and a *Multiplication* with *B* `-1` after it. Place three *Construct Point* components: (−Half span, 0, 0), (0, 0, *Camber*) and (Half span, 0, 0), and an *Arc 3Pt* through them: the deck's centre line, slightly arched.
. Place two *Move* (Transform) components that move the centre line by *Edge Y* and by its negative (a *Multiplication* with *B* `-1`) in *Y*: the two deck edges.
. Place a *Ruled Surface* (Surface) between the two edges, and an *Extrude* (Surface) with a *Vector XYZ* of *Z* `-1.5`: the deck slab.

=== Hangers

[start=8]
. Place a *Range* (Maths), _Hanger t_, with *S* `0.12`, *E* `0.88` and *Hangers → N*. Place an *Addition* and an *Expression* `x - y` that add and subtract *Hanger slope*.
. Place three *Evaluate Curve* (Curve) components:
** _On arch_: *Arc → C*, *Hanger t → t*.
** _On deck +_: the first deck edge *→ C*, *t + slope → t*.
** _On deck −_: the same edge, *t − slope → t*.
. Place two *Line* (Curve) components, _Hangers 1_ (*On arch → A*, *On deck + → B*) and _Hangers 2_ (*On arch → A*, *On deck − → B*). The two families lean opposite ways and cross.
. Place three *Pipe* (Surface) components: the arch with *Arch radius → R*, and each hanger family with *R* `0.22`.
. Place three *Mirror* (Transform) components with *N* set to `0, 1, 0` and wire the three Pipes into them: the second arch and its hangers, mirrored in the deck's centre plane.
. Turn menu:Preview[] off on the points, curves, deck edges, Ruled Surface, Evaluate Curves and Lines.

== What is happening

*Evaluate Curve* takes a normalised parameter: 0 at a curve's start, 1 at its end. Evaluating the arch and the deck edge at the same list of values gives matching points along both, whatever their shapes, and offsetting the deck's values by ± _slope_ tilts each family of hangers. That is how network arches work: crossing hangers turn the arch and deck into a stiff truss.

The geometry tests check that every hanger starts on the arch and ends on the deck edge, and that the mirrored arch is the exact reflection of the first. The whole bridge updates in about a third of a second.

== Things to try

* Set *Hanger slope* to 0 for vertical hangers (both families then coincide), or to 0.12 for a dense web. It stops at 0.12 so that t ± slope stays between 0 and 1, on the deck.
* Set *Lean* to 0 for upright twin arches, or to 8, half the deck width, and the crowns meet over the centre line. Beyond that the arches cross.
* Raise *Rise* to 70 for a dramatic arch, or *Span* to 200 for a long one.
* Delete the three Mirrors for a single-arch bridge.
