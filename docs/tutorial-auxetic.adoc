= Tutorial 36: An auxetic lattice
:page-id: tutorial-auxetic
:stem: latexmath
include::_attributes.adoc[]

[.lead]
Most materials get thinner when you stretch them. A honeycomb whose cells are bow-ties instead of hexagons (a _re-entrant_ honeycomb) gets _wider_: its Poisson's ratio is negative, which makes it _auxetic_. This example cuts such a lattice from a block, with wall thickness and cell angle on sliders, and shows the Poisson's ratio predicted by the classic bending model in a *Panel*.

image::auxetic-render.png[A blue block cut into a lattice of interlocking bow-tie cells]

Reference: choose menu:Examples[Auxetic lattice] on the canvas toolbar to load the finished definition.

== Build it

=== One cell

. Place seven sliders: _Wall length: 6 (3 – 12)_, _Rib length: 12 (4 – 24)_, _Wall angle: -30 (-40 – 40)_ with Decimals `0`, _Wall thickness: 0.8 (0.3 – 2)_ with Decimals `2`, _Columns: 7 (2 – 14)_ and _Rows: 4 (1 – 10)_ with Decimals `0`, and _Depth: 8 (2 – 30)_.
. Place two *Expression* (Maths) components, _Wall X_ `x*cos(radians(y))` and _Wall Y_ `x*sin(radians(y))`, with *Wall length → x* and *Wall angle → y*: how far an inclined wall reaches across and up.
. Place a *Multiplication* (Maths), *Rib length* × `0.5`, and an *Addition* (Maths) of that and _Wall Y_: the height of the cell's apex. Place three *Multiplication* components with *B* `-1` for the negatives of _Wall X_, the half rib and the apex.
. Place six *Construct Point* (Vector) components, the cell's corners in order: (Wall X, −half rib), (Wall X, half rib), (0, apex), (−Wall X, half rib), (−Wall X, −half rib), (0, −apex).
. Place a *Polyline* (Curve), _Cell_, with *C* set to True, and wire the six points into its *V* in order (hold kbd:[Shift] from the second on).
. Place a *Multiplication*, *Wall thickness* × `-0.5`, and an *Offset Curve* (Curve), _Hole_, with the cell and that distance: each hole is the cell shrunk by half a wall.
. Place a *Planar Surface* (Surface), a *Move* (Transform) with *Z* `-1`, an *Addition* (*Depth* + `2`), a *Vector XYZ* (Vector) with it as *Z*, and an *Extrude* (Surface), _Cutter_: a prism a little taller than the block.

=== The lattice

[start=8]
. Place an *Addition*, *Rib length* + _Wall Y_: the row spacing. Place a *Move*, _Second row_, with the cutter, _Wall X_ *→ X* and the row spacing *→ Y*: the cell that interlocks with the first.
. Place a *Linear Array* (Transform), _Columns_, and wire the cutter and the second row into its *G*, with a step of 2 × _Wall X_ along X and *Columns → N*. Place another, _Rows_, on the result, with a step of 2 × row spacing along Y and *Rows → N*.
. Place three *Expression* components: _Block width_ `2*(x - 1)*y` (*Columns*, _Wall X_), _Block height_ `(2*x - 1)*y` (*Rows*, row spacing) and _Block X_ `(x - 0.5)*y` (*Columns*, _Wall X_); and a *Multiplication*, block height × `0.5`.
. Place a *Center Box* (Surface), _Block_, with the width, height and *Depth*, and a *Move* by _Block X_ and half the height.
. Place a *Solid Difference* (Intersect), _Lattice_, with the block *→ A* and the rows *→ B*, with menu:Flatten[] on *B*: all the holes in one cut.
. For the Poisson's ratio, place an *Expression*, `cos(radians(z))**2/((x/y + sin(radians(z)))*sin(radians(z)))`, with *Rib length → x*, *Wall length → y* and *Wall angle → z*, and a *Panel* (Params).
. Turn menu:Preview[] off on everything except the Cell and the Lattice.

== What is happening

With a negative wall angle the apexes point into the cell, giving a bow-tie. Neighbouring cells share walls, and offsetting each hole by half a wall leaves walls of exactly the chosen thickness; the geometry tests check that gap. The block's edges cut through cells, not along walls or through corners, which keeps the Boolean reliable.

Pull the lattice along X and the inclined walls bend. If each wall bends by a small amount δ, the cell gets longer by δ·sin θ over a width of _l_·cos θ, and its height changes by −δ·cos θ over _h_ + _l_·sin θ. The ratio of the two strains is the Poisson's ratio for a pull along X:

[stem]
++++
\nu_{xy} = \frac{\cos^2\theta}{\left(h/l + \sin\theta\right)\sin\theta}
++++

This is the bending-only model of Gibson and Ashby's _Cellular Solids_ (chapter 4). With _h_/_l_ = 2 and θ = −30° it gives exactly −1: stretch the lattice by 1 % and it gets 1 % wider. With θ = +30° it gives +0.6, an ordinary honeycomb. The model counts only the bending of the walls; stretching and shear in real walls change the value. At exactly 0° the walls are flat and the formula has no value, so the Expression turns red.

The lattice updates in about two seconds (1.8 and 2.5 s in two test runs): one Boolean with 56 cutters. Lower *Columns* and *Rows* while you experiment.

== Things to try

* Drag *Wall angle* from −30 to +30 and watch the cells turn from bow-ties into ordinary hexagons and the Panel change sign.
* Set *Rib length* to 18: the ratio becomes −0.6.
* Set *Columns* to 4 and *Rows* to 3 for faster updates.
* Set *Rib length* to 8: the bow-ties pinch almost shut at their apexes and the ratio reaches −1.8.
