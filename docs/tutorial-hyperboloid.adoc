= Tutorial 11: A Shukhov tower
:page-id: tutorial-hyperboloid
include::_attributes.adoc[]

[.lead]
In 1896 Vladimir Shukhov built the first hyperboloid tower: a curved lattice made entirely of straight steel bars. Join each point of a lower ring to a point a few places round on an upper ring, and the straight lines sweep out a curved waist. You will make the struts with *Shift List*, then use a data tree to trace horizontal rings through every strut at once.

image::hyperboloid-render.png[A red lattice tower of straight struts, narrowing to a waist, with white rings]

Reference: choose menu:Examples[Shukhov tower (hyperboloid)] on the canvas toolbar to load the finished definition.

== Build it

=== Two rings of points

. Place seven sliders: _Struts: 24 (8 – 72)_, _Twist: 5 (1 – 20)_ and _Rings: 7 (0 – 15)_ with Decimals `0`; _Height: 120 (40 – 200)_, _Base radius: 30 (10 – 60)_, _Top radius: 16 (5 – 50)_, and _Strut radius: 0.7 (0.2 – 2)_ with Decimals `2`.
. Place two *Circle* (Curve) components, _Base ring_ and _Top ring_. Wire *Base radius → R* on the first; *Top radius → R* and *Height → Z* on the second.
. Place two *Divide Curve* (Curve) components. Wire each Circle into its own *C* and *Struts → N* on both: 24 points round each ring.

=== Struts

[start=4]
. Place a *Shift List* (Sets). Wire the top *Divide Curve P → L* and *Twist → S*. The list of top points now starts five places further round.
. Place a *Line* (Curve). Wire the bottom *Divide Curve P → A* and *Shift List L → B*. Each line climbs from a bottom point to the top point five places round: 24 struts leaning one way.
. Place a *Multiplication* (Maths) with *B* set to `-1` and wire *Twist → A*. Place a second *Shift List* and a second *Line*, wired the same way but shifting by the negative twist. These 24 struts lean the other way.

=== Rings through the struts

[start=7]
. Place an *Addition* (Maths) with *B* set to `1` and wire *Rings → A*. Place a *Range* (Maths), _Ring heights_, and wire *Addition R → N*. It gives 9 values from 0 to 1.
. Place an *Evaluate Curve* (Curve). Wire the first *Line L → C* and *Range R → t*, then tick menu:Graft[] on *t*. Every height gets its own branch, and in each branch all 24 struts are evaluated at that height: 9 branches of 24 points.
. Place an *Interpolate* (Curve) and set value its *P* (periodic) to `True`. Wire *Evaluate Curve P → V*: one smooth closed ring per branch.

=== Steel

[start=10]
. Place three *Pipe* (Surface) components: one for each Line and one for the rings. Wire *Strut radius → R* on the two strut pipes. For the rings, place a *Multiplication* with *B* set to `1.4`, wire *Strut radius → A* and its *R → R*, so the rings are a little heavier.
. Turn menu:Preview[] off on the Circles, Divide Curves, Shift Lists, Lines, Evaluate Curve and Interpolate.

== What is happening

Two lists of 24 points are paired item by item, so shifting one list is all it takes to tilt every strut. With a twist of 5 out of 24 each strut turns 75° on its way up. The struts never bend, yet together they lie on a curved surface, a _hyperboloid of one sheet_: a doubly ruled surface, which is why the two families of straight lines both fit it exactly.

The rings show the data-tree pattern at its simplest. Without Graft, Evaluate Curve would pair the 24 struts with the 9 heights item by item. With Graft every height is its own branch, and each branch evaluates all 24 struts. Because the tower is a surface of revolution, the points at any one height lie on a circle; the geometry tests check exactly that.

Each update takes about 0.8 s, mostly to mesh the 57 pipes for the preview.

== Things to try

* Drag *Twist*. At 1 the tower is almost a cone; as the twist grows the waist pulls in. Beyond half the struts (12 here) the struts lean the other way and the waist opens again.
* Set *Top radius* equal to *Base radius* for a symmetric waist, as in Shukhov's water towers.
* Set *Struts* to 48 for a fine mesh, or to 12 for a bold lattice.
* Stack two towers: place the second on top with a *Move* by *Height*, as in Shukhov's radio tower in Moscow.
