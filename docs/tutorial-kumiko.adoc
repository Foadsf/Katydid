= Tutorial 42: A kumiko asanoha panel
:page-id: tutorial-kumiko
include::_attributes.adoc[]

[.lead]
Kumiko is Japanese lattice woodwork assembled from thin strips without nails. _Asanoha_, the hemp-leaf pattern, splits every triangle of a triangular grid with three spokes from its centre to its corners. Around every grid point the spokes form a six-pointed star. Here the panel is built in one 60° sector and turned six times inside a hexagonal frame, with three strip depths.

image::kumiko-render.png[A hexagonal timber panel filled with the asanoha star pattern in strips of three depths]

Reference: choose menu:Examples[Kumiko asanoha panel] on the canvas toolbar to load the finished definition.

== Build it

=== The grid in one sector

. Place six sliders: _Cells: 3 (2 – 6)_ with Decimals `0`, _Cell size: 12 (6 – 30)_, _Frame width: 3 (1 – 8)_, _Frame depth: 12 (4 – 30)_, _Grid depth: 8 (2 – 30)_ and _Spoke depth: 5 (1 – 30)_.
. Place a *Multiplication* (Maths), *Cells* × *Cell size* (the sector's side _R_), and an *Expression* (Maths), _Row height_, `x*sqrt(3)/2`, with *Cell size*.
. Place a *Series* (Sets), _Line no._, with *Cells → C*, a *Multiplication* of it by the row height, and two *Expression* components, _Line start_ `x*y/2` (line no., *Cell size*) and _Line end_ `z*(y - x/2)` (line no., *Cells*, *Cell size*). Place two *Construct Point* (Vector) components and a *Line* (Curve): the grid lines parallel to the sector's base.
. Place a *Construct Point* at the sector's centre (_R_/2, `x/(2*sqrt(3))` of _R_), a *Series*, `0`, `120`, `3`, and a *Rotate* (Transform), _Three families_, about that centre with menu:Graft[] on *A*: the sector is an equilateral triangle, so turning one family by 120° and 240° gives the other two.

=== The spokes in one sector

[start=5]
. Place a *Multiplication*, *Cells* × *Cells*, and a *Series*, _Triangle no._, with it as *C*. Triangle _q_ sits in row ⌊_q_/*Cells*⌋ at position _q_ % *Cells*.
. Place two *Expression* components, _Up triangle?_ `max(0, 1 - floor((x % y + floor(x/y))/y))` and _Down triangle?_ `max(0, 1 - floor((x % y + floor(x/y) + 1)/y))`, with the triangle numbers and *Cells*, and two *Cull Pattern* (Sets): keep the triangles that fit in the sector.
. Place four *Expression* components for their centres, with the kept numbers, *Cells* and *Cell size*: `z*(x % y + floor(x/y)/2 + 0.5)` and `z*sqrt(3)/2*(floor(x/y) + 1/3)` for the up triangles, `z*(x % y + floor(x/y)/2 + 1)` and `z*sqrt(3)/2*(floor(x/y) + 2/3)` for the down ones.
. Place an *Expression*, _Spoke length_, `x/sqrt(3)`, a *Construct Point* at (length, 0, 0) and a *Line* from the origin to it: one spoke pointing along X.
. Place two *Series*, `90`, `120`, `3` and `30`, `120`, `3`, and two *Rotate* components of the spoke with menu:Graft[] on *A*: the three spokes of an up triangle and of a down triangle. Place two *Move* (Transform) components to put them at the centres.

=== Six sectors, strips and frame

[start=10]
. Place a *Series*, _Sector angles_, `0`, `60`, `6`, and two *Rotate* components: the three families, and the up and down spokes wired into one *G*, each with menu:Flatten[] on *G* and menu:Graft[] on *A*.
. Place three *Vector XYZ* (Vector) components with the three depths as *Z*, and two *Extrude* (Surface) components, _Grid strips_ and _Spoke strips_.
. For the frame, place an *Expression*, `x + y*2/sqrt(3)`, with _R_ and *Frame width*, two *Regular Polygon* (Curve) components with *S* `6` (radius _R_ and that), a *Ruled Surface* (Surface) between them and an *Extrude*, _Frame_, by the frame depth.
. Turn menu:Preview[] off on everything except the strips and the frame.

== What is happening

The hexagon splits into six equilateral sectors, and each sector into *Cells*² small triangles: rows of up-pointing triangles (_i_ + _j_ ≤ *Cells* − 1) with down-pointing ones between them (_i_ + _j_ ≤ *Cells* − 2). The two *Cull Pattern* formulas are those two tests written with `floor`, because *Expression* has no comparisons: `max(0, 1 - floor(s/y))` is 1 when _s_ < _y_ and 0 otherwise. A spoke runs from a triangle's centre to a corner, so each spoke is _a_/√3 long and its outer end is a grid point. The geometry tests check both of these for every spoke, and that all of them lie inside the hexagon. Turning a spoke the wrong way makes them fail.

The strips are ribbons standing on the panel's back face, so the three depths read as three levels from the front, as the frame, the grid (_jigumi_) and the infill pieces of a kumiko panel often differ in depth. The panel updates in about a third of a second.

== Things to try

* Set *Cells* to 5 for a fine pattern of 450 spokes.
* Set *Spoke depth* to 8, equal to *Grid depth*, for a flush face.
* Set *Frame width* to 8 for a heavy border.
