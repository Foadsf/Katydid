= Tutorial 20: A carved monolith
:page-id: tutorial-monolith
include::_attributes.adoc[]

[.lead]
A solid block hollowed by spherical voids that grow towards one corner, as if eroded. It is the attractor idea again, this time driving solid Booleans: sixty spheres, sized by their distance to a point, subtracted from a box in a single *Solid Difference*.

image::monolith-render.png[A sandstone block eroded by spherical voids that grow towards one corner]

Reference: choose menu:Examples[Carved monolith] on the canvas toolbar to load the finished definition.

== Build it

=== Block and void centres

. Place ten sliders: _Width: 60 (30 – 120)_, _Depth: 40 (20 – 80)_, _Height: 30 (15 – 60)_; _Voids: 60 (10 – 150)_ and _Seed: 4 (1 – 50)_ with Decimals `0`; _Smallest void: 3 (1 – 8)_ and _Largest void: 9 (3 – 15)_ with Decimals `2`; and _Attractor X: -30 (-60 – 60)_, _Attractor Y: -20 (-40 – 40)_, _Attractor Z: 30 (0 – 60)_.
. Place a *Center Box* (Surface) and wire *Width → X*, *Depth → Y* and *Height → Z*. It is centred in X and Y and stands on Z = 0.
. Place a *Populate 2D* (Vector) with *R* set to `1`. Wire *Width → W*, *Depth → H*, *Voids → N* and *Seed → S*: void positions spread over the block's footprint.
. Place a *Deconstruct* (Vector) and wire *Populate P → P*.
. Place an *Expression* (Maths), _Heights_, with *F* set to `z*(0.1 + 0.8*abs(sin(x*1.7 + y*2.3)))`. Wire *Deconstruct X → x*, *Y → y* and *Height → z*: a repeatable "random" height between 10 % and 90 % of the block for each void.
. Place a *Construct Point* (Vector), _Void centres_, and wire *X*, *Y* and *Heights* into it.

=== Void sizes

[start=7]
. Place a *Construct Point*, _Attractor_, and wire the three attractor sliders into it: by default the top of one corner.
. Place a *Distance* (Vector) and wire *Void centres → A* and *Attractor → B*.
. Place a *Bounds* (Maths) with menu:Flatten[] on *N*, wire *Distance → N*, and a *Remap Numbers* (Maths). Wire *Distance → V*, *Bounds Min → S0*, *Bounds Max → S1*, *Largest void → T0* and *Smallest void → T1*: the nearest void gets the largest radius.

=== Carving

[start=10]
. Place a *Sphere* (Surface) and wire *Remap R → R*: 60 spheres at the origin.
. Place a *Move* (Transform), _Voids_. Wire *Sphere → G*, *Deconstruct X → X*, *Deconstruct Y → Y* and *Heights → Z*.
. Place a *Solid Difference* (Intersect). Wire *Box → A* and *Voids G → B*.
. Turn menu:Preview[] off on everything except the Solid Difference.

== What is happening

The Solid Difference takes the box and all sixty spheres at once (its *B* input has list access), so OpenCascade sorts out every intersection between the voids in one operation. Spheres that overlap merge into larger caverns; spheres that cross a face of the block leave craters. The geometry tests check the volume against an independent calculation: the block minus its overlap with all the spheres fused together.

With more or larger voids near the corner, the cavities can cut thin pieces right off the block, and the result is several solids. The default settings give one; more aggressive ones may not.

If a Boolean component turns orange with "The Boolean result is not a valid solid", see xref:troubleshooting.adoc[Troubleshooting]. Each update takes about two and a half seconds, almost all of it in the Boolean.

== Things to try

* Drag *Seed* for a different pattern of voids.
* Move the attractor to the centre of the top face (*X* 0, *Y* 0) for a crater.
* Swap the Remap targets (*Smallest void → T0*, *Largest void → T1*) and the block erodes from the far corner instead.
* Replace the Sphere with a *Center Box* for cubic voids, or with a *Cylinder* for drilled holes.
